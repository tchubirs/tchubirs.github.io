## Elevator pitch (máx. 200 caracteres)

> The oldest game there is, in VR: make shadow animals with your hands, and when the
> shape is right, the shadow comes alive and walks off the wall. Hands only. No menus.

## About the project

### Inspiration

Every child has done it: hands between a lamp and a wall, and a rabbit appears.
Hand tracking on Quest finally sees every joint of your fingers — so the headset can
tell when your shadow *is* a rabbit, and let it hop away.

### What it does

You sit facing a paper wall. A lantern warms up behind you. Raise your hands and
their shadows fall on the wall — finger by finger, the real shadow of your real
hands.

The wall teaches you four classic shadow figures: a **rabbit**, a **dog**, a
**bird** and a **crocodile**. Next to the request, the faint shadow of the right hand shape breathes on
the wall. If you get stuck, the wall tells you **one thing only — the thing that is
wrong**: *"Tuck your thumb over them."* *"Drop your little finger — the mouth."*
*"Hook your thumbs together."*

When the shape is right, a chime rings and your shadow comes alive: the rabbit
twitches its ears and hops off, the dog barks and trots away wagging its tail, the
bird beats its wings and flies up the wall, the crocodile snaps three times and crawls away. Each sound comes from where the animal
is. After the four, the wall says *"Now the light is yours."*

No controllers, no menus, no buttons — nothing to install. It runs in the Quest
browser from a link.

### How we built it

- **WebXR + three.js**, hand tracking with all 25 joints per hand. The hand shadow is
  built from the joints themselves (a sphere per joint, a capsule per bone) and cast
  by a real spotlight onto the wall — there is no hand model.
- **The figure is judged by its shadow, not by the hand.** The recogniser projects
  the joints onto the wall plane: a finger pointed at the wall is an ear in 3D but a
  dot on the wall, and it does not count.
- **The same rules recognise the figure and write the hint**, so the two never
  disagree.
- **All sound is synthesised in code** — the lantern crackle, the chime, the hop, the
  bark, the chirp, the wings, the snap. No audio files, nothing third-party. Positional (HRTF).
- **The shadows that come alive are hands too** — classic two-hand shadow puppets
  (dog, rabbit, bird, crocodile), animated joint by joint. Every 1/30 s of every
  puppet's life is checked in code to be something two real hands can do: joint
  angles within anatomical limits, at most one left and one right hand, no finger
  passing through another, the two hands and forearms never passing through each
  other, wrists within limits, arms coming from the player's body, and each hand
  within reach of its shoulder. 17,568 puppet positions, all possible.
- **All art is code**: rice-paper texture, dust drifting in the beam, a hand shadow
  with a real palm and the skin between thumb and index.
- Built and tested without a headset, in Meta's **IWER** emulator running an
  emulated Quest 3 inside a headless browser.

### Measured, not claimed

- The same feasibility check caught two of our own taught poses: the rabbit's thumb
  sank 9 mm into the ring finger, and our first bird needed both forearms in the same
  place. The bird is now the classic crossed-wrist bird.
- Every test hand is built from **joint angles within the limits of a real hand**,
  with bone lengths measured on the real captured hand that ships with IWER. An
  impossible pose cannot be written — the builder refuses it.
- 27 of 27 fixed cases: the four figures, and **19 hands that are not figures** —
  including the three real captured poses shipped with IWER (rest, pinch, point) and
  the near-misses (rabbit pointed at the wall, "three" with the thumb out, dog with
  its mouth closed, bird with the thumbs apart…).
- Robustness, 2000 hands per case, rotated and trembling: up to 20° and 4 mm of
  tracking noise, **96–100% recognised, and the worst false positive is 0.3%**. At 30°
  and 6 mm the dog drops to 55% and the bird and crocodile to 38% — which is why the
  game teaches the pose.
- Every rule was broken on purpose to check that a test catches it.
- 21 of 21 near-misses receive the right hint.

### Challenges

Deciding by the **shadow** instead of the hand. And false positives: a figure that
lights up when you did not make it ruins the magic, so every rule had to reject
real, relaxed hands first.

### What we learned

A measurement is only as honest as its reference: several of our own "improvements"
were rejected by the test bench — including one rule that looked useless in the fixed
tests but kept false positives at 0.3% instead of 2.0% once the hand tilts.

### What's next

More figures (the snail, the deer), a daily shadow to come back for,
and — the one thing we could not do — hours of play with real hands on a real
headset.

### Honest note

This is a solo project, built with AI coding assistance. It was developed and tested
entirely in the emulator; **it has not yet been played on a physical headset**.
